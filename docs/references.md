# Methods and biological context

These papers supply established methods and comparison context. They do not
validate IntraPhy's accuracy or imply that a broad discrete-state likelihood
method is new.

- Felsenstein J. (1981). Evolutionary trees from DNA sequences: a maximum
  likelihood approach. *Journal of Molecular Evolution* 17:368–376.
  [doi:10.1007/BF01734359](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01734359).
  Tree likelihood and pruning.
- Lewis PO. (2001). A likelihood approach to estimating phylogeny from
  discrete morphological character data. *Systematic Biology* 50:913–925.
  [doi:10.1080/106351501753462876](https://doi.org/10.1080/106351501753462876).
  Discrete-character likelihood and variable-character ascertainment.
- Csűrös M. (2008). Malin: maximum likelihood analysis of intron evolution in
  eukaryotes. *Bioinformatics* 24:1538–1539.
  [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btn226](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btn226).
  Intron-position gain/loss inference; its binary intron-site process is a
  distinct biological model from source-qualified DNA-copy transitions.
- Csűrös M, Holey JA, Rogozin IB. (2007). In search of lost introns.
  *Bioinformatics*. [doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btm190](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btm190).
  Observed-position ascertainment in intron evolution.
- Nguyen HD, Yoshihama M, Kenmochi N. (2005). New maximum likelihood
  estimators for eukaryotic intron evolution. *PLoS Computational Biology*
  1(7):e79. [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.0010079](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.0010079).
  Definition of intron gain/loss opportunities and comparative likelihood.
- Márquez Y, Mantica F, Cozzuto L, et al. (2021). ExOrthist: a tool to infer
  exon orthologies at any evolutionary distance. *Genome Biology* 22:239.
  [doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02441-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02441-9).
  Sequence and intron-context evidence for exon correspondence.
- Lee C, Kim N, Roy M, Graveley BR. (2010). Massive expansions of Dscam
  splicing diversity via staggered homologous recombination during arthropod
  evolution. *RNA* 16:91–105.
  [doi:10.1261/rna.1812710](https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.1812710).
  Biological context for a synthetic Dscam-like copy example; splicing
  evolution is outside the current DNA-copy likelihood.
